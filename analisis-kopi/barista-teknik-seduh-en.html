Baristas and Brew Technique: from Tamping to Machine Care

AEKI breaks espresso-machine upkeep into component-by-component steps—purge spent grounds, rinse the portafilter and group head, clean the steam wand, wash the drip tray and removable parts—with two caveats: only backflush if the model and manual say so, and treat descaling as a separate job [2]. On prep, AEKI stresses leveling grounds, keeping the tamper parallel, and a single stable press with a neutral wrist; flatness matters more than chasing a pressure number [4].

Repeatable technique

Setting aside the matcha-versus-coffee debate that lacks a neat yes/no [1], a barista’s day is built on small, consistent motions. Good tamping starts with even distribution in the basket, a parallel tamper, and one stable press with a neutral wrist. The priority is a flat surface; chasing a specific pressure matters less than avoiding a tilted surface that can leave parts of the puck denser than others [4]. The move is quick, so repetition and checking the result are what make it reliable during rushes [4].

Clear machine care

Machine cleanliness decides whether the gear is ready for service again. AEKI’s checklist lays out a simple order: dump grounds, rinse the portafilter and group head, clean the steam wand, wash the drip tray and any removable parts [2]. It also separates two tasks: backflush only if the model and manual support it, while descaling addresses mineral buildup and is a different job [2]. The first step before touching the machine is identifying the model and reading the care-and-cleaning section of the manual [2].

Hiring and training the people

Entry-level barista interviews typically assess three things: why you applied, evidence of relevant ability, and how you behave in a service role [3]. Beginners don’t need to pretend experience; honest, specific answers with small examples are more convincing than big claims [3]. This framing helps trainers build habits—not scripts—because interviewers can change question order or probe for detail [3].

Conclusion

The throughline is simple: repeatable basics, manual-driven maintenance, and an interview lens that looks for real evidence. That’s enough to run a tidy bar without leaning on gear myths or fuzzy health claims.
