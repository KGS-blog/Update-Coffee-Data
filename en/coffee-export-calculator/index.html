Q.co FOB & Landed Coffee Calculator: features and method

The Q.co calculator helps coffee exporters prepare indicative Indonesian coffee prices from market references through FOB and landed cost at US ports. It combines market-reference updates, product and cost inputs, a market-range check, quality and origin comparisons, and printable quotation generation.

1. Refreshable and editable market references

The World Coffee Prices panel shows Arabica C-Market from Yahoo Finance, the International Coffee Organization (ICO) daily Robusta indicator, and the rupiah exchange rate from a data feed. Refresh loads the latest available reference data. Users can adjust reference prices, exchange rate, Arabica and Robusta differentials, and cherry ratio before running a simulation. The panel also displays per-kilogram equivalents and indicative specialty premiums by Q-Grade range.

Values shown before the feed finishes loading are simulation defaults. The ICO Robusta indicator represents the Robusta group; it is not an ICE futures quote. Review the source and update time before using the estimate.

2. FOB estimate by origin, grade, volume, and cost

Select an Indonesian coffee origin, commercial or specialty grade, volume, Indonesian FOB port, and IDR/USD exchange rate. Grade options include G1, G1+, G2, G3, several Q-Grade levels, and micro-lot. The calculator recommends a differential based on origin and grade. The differential and quality premium remain editable to reflect the lot and buyer agreement.

Users can adjust processing and quality-control costs, packaging, export documents, domestic logistics, pallet price, pallet capacity, and margin. Local costs are converted from IDR to USD per kilogram. Pallet cost per kilogram is calculated from pallet price divided by its capacity.

In brief: add the coffee reference price per kilogram, differential, and quality premium; add local per-kilogram costs converted to USD; then apply the margin to that subtotal. The FOB price per kilogram multiplied by shipment volume gives the total FOB value.

3. Landed-cost estimate to the United States

Choose a destination port: New York/Baltimore, Los Angeles/Long Beach, Miami/Houston, or Seattle/Tacoma. Shipment mode is estimated from volume: LCL below 10 metric tons, FCL 20-foot from 10–21 metric tons, and FCL 40-foot above 21 metric tons. Pallet count is also estimated from volume; the standard assumption is about 1,000 kg per pallet and can be adjusted.

Destination-cost breakdown includes ocean freight, customs broker, Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF), Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF), and cargo insurance. Estimated landed cost per kilogram adds these components to FOB per kilogram, then calculates the shipment total. Freight rates and destination costs are estimates that can change; replace them with current quotes. Items such as FDA Prior Notice, required fumigation, demurrage, US inland transport, and storage need separate confirmation.

4. Market Reality Check

Results show the coffee reference, differential, local costs, pallet, margin, FOB, and landed breakdown. Market Reality Check compares the FOB estimate with an indicative range derived from the reference and local costs, then signals PASAR, UNDERVALUE, or OVERVALUE. This is an automatic comparison aid, not a transaction appraisal or price guarantee.

5. Multiple products and automatic quotation

After calculating a product, users can add it to the FOB Product List. The list records origin, grade, volume, exchange rate, FOB price, and landed costs for each product; products can be removed individually or the list can be cleared. Once products have been added, Generate Quotation automatically builds a quotation from the list.

The quotation summarizes the date, origin and destination ports, exchange rate and market references, total volume, estimated pallets and shipment mode, shared landed costs, and a per-product table with volume, FOB/kg, landed/kg, and FOB and landed totals. All products in one quotation must use the same origin and destination ports. The result can be printed or saved as a PDF using the browser print dialog. Review prices, costs, payment terms, validity, and printed conditions before sending it to a buyer; it is an estimate draft to be adjusted to the actual transaction.

6. Convert Q-Grade to an indicative premium

The Grade Calculator accepts a 70–95 cupping score through a slider or number input. It shows a quality classification, indicative premium and differential ranges, C-Market reference per kilogram, estimated FOB range, and a potentially relevant buyer segment. These values are for simulation; cupping results and the agreed lot terms remain the basis for pricing.

7. Compare Indonesian coffee origins

The origin comparison table gives indicative altitude, processing, score range, flavor profile, and FOB range for selected Indonesian origins. Filters cover Sumatra, Sulawesi, Bali and NTT, and Java and Papua. These ranges are context for comparison, not current transaction listings.

Usage guide and estimate limitations

The calculator’s step-by-step guide explains how to refresh market references, choose an origin and grade, edit costs, set volume and ports, review results, and create a quotation. The tool is intended for planning and comparison. Results are not a price offer, quality appraisal, profit guarantee, or substitute for confirming market prices, exchange rates, freight, taxes, insurance, and contract requirements.

Reference sources: Yahoo Finance for Arabica C-Market, the ICO daily Robusta indicator, and the exchange-rate feed used by the calculator. ICO public market information. See Kabar Kopi Data & Trends.
